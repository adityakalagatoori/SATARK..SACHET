The Problem

A mule account looks exactly like a good customer
— until the money has already moved.

Mule accounts are the plumbing of financial fraud. They receive proceeds from phishing, investment scams and card compromise, then layer and move those funds across UPI, IMPS, NEFT and RTGS rails fast enough that recovery windows close before a case is even opened.

The accounts themselves are unremarkable on paper: legitimate KYC, ordinary balances, ordinary tenure. What distinguishes them is behaviour over time — pass-through velocity, balance that never rests, structuring just under reporting thresholds, and bursts of counterparty churn. Static rule thresholds cannot encode that, so they compensate by flagging broadly.

The needle

Just 81 of 9,082 accounts are confirmed mules — a 0.89% prevalence. A model that predicts "clean" for every single account is still 99.11% accurate, which is why accuracy is a meaningless target here.

The haystack

3,924 pre-aggregated behavioural features per account — cash, cheque, UPI, NEFT and RTGS ratios across multiple time windows, balance deviations and tenure. No analyst reviews that by hand, at that volume.

The false-positive tax

Rule engines flag cheaply and push the cost of precision onto humans. A Current/MSME account legitimately runs high throughput on a low balance — the exact pass-through signature — so naive rules bury real cases under look-alike noise.

Investigator Workflow

How SACHET works, alert to resolution

Four steps, from the moment your existing rule engine fires to the moment a case is closed. SACHET only owns step two — everything else is your process, unchanged.

  1. 01

    Alert flagged

    Your existing rule engine fires as it does today — threshold breaches, velocity rules, watchlist hits. Nothing about that layer changes, and nothing is suppressed.

  2. 02

    Scored by SACHET

    The stacked ensemble scores the account out-of-fold — LightGBM, NNPU, CatBoost and the ECOD / Deep Isolation Forest pair, combined by the meta-learner into one calibrated mule-risk probability.

  3. 03

    Queue sorted

    Alerts are ranked by score and tiered by severity, so the top of the queue is the part actually worth opening. At Precision@100 = 64%, roughly two in three of the first hundred reviews land on a confirmed mule.

  4. 04

    Resolved

    The investigator works the ranked list with SHAP reasons attached to each case, and the outcome feeds back for continuous retraining.

Stage two
Architecture

Four models that disagree usefully

Each learner is included because it fails differently from the others. The meta-learner is what turns that disagreement into signal.

Model B · Supervised

Cost-sensitive LightGBM

Gradient boosting with scale_pos_weight ≈ 111 to counter the 99:1 imbalance directly in the loss rather than by resampling. The strongest single model in the ensemble.

OOF AUC-PR · 0.800
Model A · Positive-Unlabeled

NNPU learner

A custom non-negative PU objective (Kiryo et al., NeurIPS 2017) that treats the 9,001 unconfirmed accounts as unlabeled, not clean. Without this, the model is trained to agree that every mule the bank hasn't caught yet is legitimate.

OOF AUC-PR · 0.75
Model E · Supervised

CatBoost

Included for ordered boosting, which reduces target-leakage risk when the positive set is this small. Earns a positive learned meta-weight alongside LightGBM.

OOF AUC-PR · 0.75
Model C · Unsupervised

ECOD + Deep Isolation Forest

Anomaly scores computed with no labels at all. Their job is not to beat the supervised models on the 81 known mules — it is to surface typologies that are absent from those 81 examples entirely.

Label-free · typology discovery
Stacking

Logistic-regression meta-learner

Fits over every base model's out-of-fold probability — the combination is learned from held-out predictions, never from in-sample ones.

    K-Means triage vs. unranked review

    Behavioural triage, without labels

    K-Means (k=5, quantile-transformed, no labels used in fitting) concentrates 71 of 81 confirmed mules — 87.7% — into a single cluster spanning 2,342 of 9,082 accounts. Investigators get a materially smaller pool to prioritise before any classifier score is applied.

    Why AUC-PR, not AUC-ROC

    Under 99:1 imbalance, ROC-AUC inflates toward the trivial-classifier value and flatters almost any model. AUC-PR is reported as the primary metric precisely because it does not. The random-baseline AUC-PR here is 0.0089.

    Why You Can Trust The Numbers

    The discipline is the contribution

    Under this much class imbalance, an undisciplined pipeline produces a spectacular score and a worthless model. Here is exactly what was done to prevent that.

    • Leakage-free nested cross-validation Mutual-information selection (top 150 of ~1,800 post-cleaning features), log / polynomial / interaction engineering and RobustScaler fitting happen only on each fold's training partition, then apply without refitting to that fold's held-out rows. A naive pipeline that selects features before splitting collapsed to a perfect AUC-PR of 1.0000 after a single boosting round — itself a diagnostic of leakage, not of quality.
    • Artifact and resolution features excluded F2230 (batch-collection month) separates accounts by when data was gathered, not by behaviour, and is unavailable at inference time. F3912 is a documented post-investigation resolution flag that correctly marks 79 of 81 mules — training on it would mean training on the answer. Both are dropped, along with the raw row index.
    • Gender excluded as a protected attribute F3892 (GENDER) is deliberately never encoded. Scoring customers for account restriction on a protected attribute is a fairness and regulatory hazard that is not defensible in a bank deployment — so it is left out regardless of any predictive value it might carry.
    • The bank's finalised features were tested, not assumed The 18 features explicitly finalised in the data dictionary (F115, F321, F527 … F3894) were evaluated directly. Forcing them into the selected set reduced out-of-fold AUC-PR from 0.80 to 0.73, so they are surfaced in the alert payload as investigator-facing context (age, tenure, balance, occupation) rather than pushed into the model as predictive signal.
    • Explanations computed after training, never fed back SHAP TreeExplainer produces per-account reasons post-training only, so explanation values are never circularly reused for feature selection.
    • No synthetic oversampling No SMOTE, no synthetic minority generation. Imbalance is handled in the loss function and in the PU formulation, so every reported result is reproducible directly from the competition dataset.
